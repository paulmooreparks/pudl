Raised means pressable

21 September 2026

Flat design took away the one cue that told a reader what on the screen would respond to a click. A label and a button became the same coloured rectangle, and people learned to hover over everything to find out which was which.

PUDL puts the cue back with three surfaces. Anything raised, lit from above with a hairline border, can be pressed. Anything sunken, with a soft inner shadow, takes input. Anything flat is there to be read. The stylesheet derives every raised and sunken treatment from four lighting tokens, so a new palette keeps the grammar without hand-tuning a single shadow.

The tokens are short enough to read in one sitting. Listing 1 shows the lighting tokens and the raised treatment they produce. Open it, then press Escape or the close button to come back here, and notice the new row under this article in the list while the listing is open.

The rule has one practical consequence that surprises people: a status badge must never look raised, however tempting it is to make it pop, because a reader will try to press it.
